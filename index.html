CourtVision

I wrote a program that turns a half-hour highlight compilation into a 30-second vertical edit.

You give it a player and a song. It picks the plays, keeps the player in frame, and cuts on the beat. These six came straight out of it.

What it's looking at

The Derrick Rose reel with the tracking drawn on. Yellow is Rose, grey is everyone else, orange is the ball, blue is the rim. The green box is the part that ends up in the final cut.

It runs four models, all on my laptop: one finds the players and the ball, one finds the rim, one catches the broadcast cutting to another camera, and one reads jersey numbers so the reel stays on the right player.

How it works

  1. Watch the whole video: crowd noise, movement, every camera cut.
  2. Find the plays: who has the ball, when they shoot, whether it goes in.
  3. Pick the best ones: no repeats, nothing that runs across a camera cut.
  4. Cut to the song: every clip is a whole number of beats, and the biggest play lands on the drop.
  5. Reframe for a phone: the crop follows the player down the court.

About 40,000 lines of Python. It runs offline, and the same video and song always give the same edit. Notes in METHOD.md and ARCHITECTURE.md.

The footage and music belong to their owners; the reels are here as examples.
